The desktop application

Your data.
Your analysis.

FT-ITC Analysis is a free, open-source desktop application for planning experiments, processing raw ITC data, fitting interaction models and presenting the results. Work locally, revisit any stage of the workflow and adapt the analysis to the needs of your data.

Core workflow

Make each analytical step inspectable.

01

Import experimental data

Open supported instrument files and review experiment details, concentrations and raw thermograms.

02

Baseline and integration

Fit baselines, adjust peak integration ranges and inspect how choices affect each injection heat.

03

Fit and compare

Fit standard interaction models, explore global or temperature-dependent parameters, and review residuals.

04

Review the analysis result

Bring related experiments together to review averaged parameters, global results and advanced analyses of temperature, salt or proton-linked effects.

05

Export the result

Create vector-based publication figures and supporting figures, then export processed results with their analytical context intact.

Capabilities

Global fitting when your question spans experiments.

Analyse related datasets together, sharing selected parameters while retaining experiment-specific values where needed.

01

Global fitting

Simultaneously fit multiple isotherms, connect parameters through physical relationships, and evaluate systematic effects across temperature, buffer or salt conditions.

02

Buffer subtraction

Apply direct, linear-fit or exponential-fit reference subtraction with propagated uncertainty.

03

Tandem merging

Combine syringe-refill experiments using standard concatenation or back-mixing compensation.

04

Figure creation

Prepare configurable vector-based figures for publication and supporting information.

05

Advanced analyses

Explore structuring, salt-dependence and proton-linkage analyses when the experiment supports them.

Local-first by design

Your experimental data remains on your computer.

FT-ITC Analysis does not require an account or upload your experimental data to an FT-ITC service. The desktop application runs locally, while the public repository makes the code and release record available for inspection.
